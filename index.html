The two rates that move Dubai.

The dirham is pegged to the US dollar. When American rates move, Gulf financing costs follow, usually within the same week. That is why a Dubai desk watches Washington.

10 Year Treasury Yield

This is the benchmark for long term money worldwide. When it rises, mortgage costs here rise with it and buyers who were stretching quietly step back. When it falls, enquiries pick up.

Dubai market, first half 2026

What the market actually did.

Every figure below comes from a published report and carries its source. No estimates and no smoothing.

Total sales value

AED 286.43 billion

Second highest first half in the history of the emirate.

For scale, that is roughly one and a half billion dirhams of property changing hands every day for six months. Depth like this is what stops a quiet period turning into a downturn.

Source: TruHauz, July 2026

Record quarter

AED 252bn

Across 60,303 transactions in Q1 2026.

The strongest quarter on record. Most of the half year value landed early, so a calmer second quarter is a return to normal rather than a warning.

Source: TruHauz, July 2026

Off plan share

67 to 71%

Of first half transaction volume.

Around two in three buyers purchase before completion and pay the developer in instalments rather than borrowing up front. That is why Dubai feels rate movements less sharply than London or New York.

Source: multiple market reports, July 2026

Apartment price index

145.19

Up from 144.10, a gain of 0.75% in the month.

Prices are still rising but gently. Under one percent in a month describes a market walking rather than sprinting, which is what you want when buying to hold.

      Analysis

      What this means for you.

      United States rates set the price of money everywhere the dollar reaches, and the dirham is tied to the dollar. So the two figures at the top of this page quietly decide what a mortgage costs here, even though neither is a Dubai number.

      Now look at the off plan share. Around seven in ten buyers this year purchased before completion and paid the developer in instalments instead of taking a large loan on day one. That is the reason Dubai kept setting records while borrowing stayed expensive. A large part of this market simply is not in the mortgage queue.

      The price index tells the same steady story. Apartments rose 0.75% in a month, which is measured rather than overheated. Put that alongside AED 286 billion of sales in six months and AED 65 billion of commercial deals and you have a market with real depth and real occupiers. Office volumes rising 38% on the year is the line I would underline, because businesses are committing to stay.

      So what would I tell you this week? If you are buying off plan, the payment plan and the developer track record matter more than the rate picture, so check both carefully. If you need a mortgage, watch the ten year yield above, because that is what moves your monthly payment, and secure pre approval while your number is known. If you are choosing between communities, read the honest case above and then speak to me about the one you are weighing.
